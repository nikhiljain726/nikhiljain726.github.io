Shorter things

Reflections on work, building, and figuring it out. Mostly saved from LinkedIn so they live somewhere I own.

2023

From internships to a super generalist

Before my first full-time role I did four very different internships, and each one shaped how I approach a new problem now.

  • Data analytics at Gionee. Sales data, inventory placement, spotting stores worth stocking. My gateway to analytics.
  • Technical intern at Phonon. Java and Asterisk, building call-centre systems. It stretched my technical and problem-solving range.
  • Technical support at Matrix Comsec. Resolving queries for offshore customers. A practical lesson in troubleshooting and service.
  • Sales trainee at BYJU'S. Door-to-door sales, learning the pulse of direct customer engagement.

Looking back, each was a stepping stone. The mix is the reason I do not fear diving into something new.

2023

What makes you stand out at work

It is not about flaunting talent. Two things do most of the work.

  • Reliability. Being dependable is invaluable. Fulfil commitments, stay consistent, and trust builds from the small everyday actions.
  • Figuring it out. Being resourceful, proactive, and adaptable, turning obstacles into growth instead of excuses.
2023

On being the youngest leader in the room

When I became Head of Marketing at Tatvic, I was one of the youngest people on the leadership team. A few things I held onto: leadership is not about age, it is about passion, dedication, and drive. I was lucky to work with a team that championed people regardless of age. Vision, innovation, and impact matter, not the number of candles on your cake.

2023

Why I like hard things

Back in 11th standard I was drawn to rotational dynamics over plain motion. I enjoyed it more, and I scored higher, because it was harder. That has been a consistent theme since. I do my best work when the problem is more intricate than usual. When something is genuinely hard, a switch flips and I get more motivated, not less.

2021

Startup vs job, one way to think about it

Say you earn 20 lakh a year. You leave to build your own thing, or you join as a co-founder. Worst case, it does not work and you shut shop in a year. In that year you learned things that would have taken ten years otherwise.

Back on the market, more companies want you, because building from the ground up is a rare skill, and the offers are often two to three times your last salary with a broader role. You created enormous value for yourself either way. Now compare: in the same job, would your salary have jumped two to three times in a year? Probably not. And if the startup works out, nothing beats it.

That is roughly my own story. Except I am still building.
