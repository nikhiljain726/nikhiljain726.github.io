What 50 crore in ad spend taught me about optimization

Performance marketing  ·  February 2026  ·  5 min read

My first real job in marketing was at Merkle Sokrati, running analytics and campaigns for top-tier clients with over 50 crore in annual ad spend between them. Managing budgets that size teaches you fast that optimization is mostly a data problem wearing a media costume.

At scale, the dashboard lies by default

When you are spending that much across accounts, no two platforms agree on what happened. Meta claims a conversion, Google claims the same one, the analytics tool disagrees with both. Left alone, you optimize toward whichever platform is most generous with credit, which is exactly the wrong incentive. Most of the actual work was reconciling numbers before touching a single bid.

Optimization is mostly a data problem wearing a media costume.

Why I built Periscope

The team was making good calls slowly, because the data to make them was scattered across tabs and exports. So I built an internal analytics tool we called Periscope. It pulled the campaign data into one place and surfaced the few numbers that actually changed a decision, so a strategist could see what to do without assembling a spreadsheet first.

The point was not a prettier report. It was shortening the distance between a signal and an action, and doing it the same way every time so decisions did not depend on who happened to build that week's sheet. That idea, turning a repeatable judgment into a tool, is one I have carried into everything since.

Three things that held up

  • Decide what you are optimizing before you optimize. ROAS, CAC, contribution margin, and blended efficiency all pull in different directions. Pick one as the boss metric and make the rest supporting.
  • Trust the number you can reconcile. A slightly worse metric you understand beats a flattering one you cannot explain. You will act on it with more confidence and less whiplash.
  • Build the tool once the judgment is stable. Do not automate a decision you are still figuring out. Once the call is repeatable, tooling turns it from a person's habit into the team's default.

Budgets have gotten bigger and the platforms have changed, but the shape of the work has not. Get the data honest, decide what matters, and make the good decision the easy one to repeat.
