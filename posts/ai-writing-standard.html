The AI writing standard I set for my team

What I expect from everyone on the team, myself first, whenever AI touches writing other people will read.

Notes  ·  Oct 2026  ·  4 min read
TL;DR

I expect everyone on my team, me first, to use AI for drafting, brainstorming, and proofing, never to decide what to say. You stand behind every sentence you publish. You spend more time authoring than your reader spends reading, because a doc is written once and read by many. If a piece is really just a prompt, share the prompt.

A note on how I expect AI to show up in the writing my team ships, and I hold myself to it first. The short version is that AI sits in the drafting, not the thinking. These are the rules, and they apply to anything other people will read.

Stand behind every sentence

If a reader points at a line and asks what it means, the one answer nobody on the team gets to give is that the AI wrote it, ignore it. The moment you publish, it stands for your thinking. If you cannot defend a sentence, it does not go in. Readers can usually tell the parts you do not mean, and the ones they cannot tell, they take as your real view. Both are a problem.

Writing is thinking, so do not skip it

Deciding what to emphasize and how to order it is where you actually learn a topic. Hand that to a model to save an hour and you come away understanding the thing less. A spec or a write-up is partly proof that you thought it through. The document is not the only point. The thinking is.

Spend more time writing than the reader spends reading

A doc is written once and read by many. If you generate a long draft from a short prompt and make people wade through it, you have pushed your work onto their time, multiplied by every reader. Paying the cost once, up front, to make it short and clear is the better trade. And if a piece turns out to be really just the prompt, share the prompt.

Where AI helps, and where it does not

AI is good at the average, competent first pass, and that is genuinely useful. It is not good at the calls that make writing worth reading, what to keep, what to cut, what to put your name on. Those stay with the person.

When you quote the machine

Sometimes raw AI output is worth sharing even when it would not pass the bar. That is fine if you label it plainly, like: the model suggested this angle, worth a look? What nobody should do is slip unedited output in and present it as their own considered view.

The model can help us write. It does not get to decide what we think.

None of this is anti-AI. We use it every day, including on this site. The bar is simple, and it holds for me as much as anyone. As these tools get better at reading intent, we will lean on them more, and the bar will still hold.
