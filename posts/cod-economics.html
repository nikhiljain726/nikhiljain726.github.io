Is COD good for end users? The hidden math of cash on delivery

D2C & founder lessons  ·  November 2021  ·  5 min read

A simple example, worked all the way through. I wrote this in 2021 while running a D2C brand, and the math still holds.

Ten parcels leave Delhi

  • Roughly 40 to 50 percent of parcels get returned to the source, depending on the industry. For this calculation, take it as 50 percent: a 50 percent delivery rate and 50 percent RTO (return to origin).
  • Ten parcels leave the warehouse in Delhi. The brand has already paid to ship 10 packages.
  • On delivery day, 5 of the 10 people refuse delivery and 5 accept.
  • The 5 refused parcels travel all the way back to the source warehouse, and the seller pays that return shipping too.
  • So the seller sold 5 products but paid transport on 15 parcels. Effective shipping cost: 3X.
  • Every customer is acquired at a marketing cost. Ten were acquired, 5 orders landed. Effective marketing cost: 2X.
The seller sold 5 products but paid to ship 15. That is the real cost of cash on delivery, and the customer pays for it.

What that does to the price

Say the product costs Rs 600, one-way transport is Rs 100, and marketing is Rs 300 per acquired customer.

To break even, the seller needs a markup of 600 + (3 x 100) + (2 x 300) = Rs 1500. Add a 10 percent margin and the MRP lands around Rs 1650.

600 (cost price) + 3 x 100 (effective shipping) + 2 x 300 (effective marketing) = Rs 1500 to break even.

What is actually happening

  • The country's logistics capacity is being spent moving parcels that will never be used.
  • The end customer pays extra, because the brand has to cover RTO losses across everyone.
  • Delivery companies earn on every leg. In some cases there are incentives that quietly push parcels toward RTO, and delivery staff get trained in ways that grow that business.
  • If you pay prepaid, you are subsidising the segment that defaults on delivery. In a fair world a prepaid customer would pay only 600 + (1 x 100) + (1 x 300) = Rs 1000, plus a 10 percent margin, about Rs 1100. That is roughly Rs 550 less than the COD price.
  • Remove COD from the large marketplaces and prices on products under Rs 2000 would likely fall 25 to 30 percent.
600 (cost price) + 100 (shipping) + 300 (marketing) = Rs 1000 for a prepaid order.

So the honest question: should COD really be the default way we let customers buy?
