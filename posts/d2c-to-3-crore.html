Bootstrapping a D2C brand to 3 crore in year one

D2C & founder lessons  ·  April 2026  ·  6 min read

Between 2020 and 2022 I ran Art Galore, a personalized-products brand I bootstrapped from nothing. In its first year it did 3 crore in revenue and served over 14,000 customers, almost all acquired through Meta and Google. This is what I would tell myself if I started it again.

The setup

Personalized products are a hard category. Every order is a little different, so you cannot just warehouse stock and ship. I set up an 1800 square foot manufacturing unit and a team of ten to actually make the things, which meant the marketing and the operations had to move at the same speed. Selling faster than you can make is not growth, it is a backlog with a refund attached.

WhatsApp was the whole store

The single best decision was running the entire customer journey on WhatsApp: discovery, order notifications, design validation, and post-purchase follow-up. For a personalized product, design validation is the tense moment. The customer needs to see and approve the artwork before it is made. Doing that over email would have killed conversion. Doing it on WhatsApp, where people already are, made it feel like a conversation instead of a form.

The vendor portal that kept privacy intact

As volume grew, vendors needed to validate designs directly with customers, but I did not want to hand out customer phone numbers. So I built a vendor management portal with no-code tools that let vendors manage orders and confirm designs with customers on WhatsApp while masking the numbers. It sounds small. It was the thing that let me add vendors without adding risk, and it is the reason the operation scaled past what I could personally touch.

What I got wrong

Product, not marketing. I was good at acquiring customers and slow at reading where the category was heading. I kept planning capacity for the products that were selling now, and got caught flat when tastes moved. The honest lesson is that in a trend-driven category, resource planning is a forecasting problem, not a fulfilment one, and I treated it as fulfilment for too long.

What carried over

The habits that made Art Galore work are the ones I still use. Acquire on the channels where intent is cheapest, run the relationship where the customer already is, and automate the boring middle so a small team can hold a large operation. That last one, letting a handful of people run something much bigger than themselves, is basically the whole job I do now, just pointed at GTM instead of mugs and frames.
