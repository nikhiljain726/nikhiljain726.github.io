Fifteen minutes of planning, up front

The smallest habit I have, and the one that has done the most for how much I get done.

Career & notes  ·  Oct 2023  ·  4 min read

You have probably heard this one before. Spend the first fifteen minutes of the day planning it. It sounds too small to matter, which is exactly why most people skip it. It is the single thing that has made me most productive, so I keep doing it.

The routine itself is three steps.

I use Google Tasks because it sits inside G Suite and there is nothing to learn. Microsoft has its own version. The simpler the tool, the more you actually use it, so pick the plain one.

The rules that make it hold up

  1. No multitasking. Plan the day in chunks, and when it is time for a task, do only that. Simple, and it is most of the benefit.
  2. Everything goes in the list. I mean everything. An email to answer later, a leave application, a note to self, a reflection. If it is not written down, it slips.
  3. Protect focus time. Block it, close the distractions, and the amount you get through in one clean stretch will surprise you.
  4. Question meetings with no agenda. If an invite arrives without one, I ask for it. It says my time has a value, and it nudges the other person to treat theirs the same way.
  5. Think in the hourly value of your time. Work out roughly what an hour of yours is worth. Once you can see the number, you spend the hours more honestly.
Fifteen minutes up front, and the day feels like mine instead of a queue of other people's requests.

That is the whole system. It is not clever and it does not need an app you have to learn. Try it for a week and see whether the day stops running you.
