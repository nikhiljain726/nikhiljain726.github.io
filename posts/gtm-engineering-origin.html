How GTM engineering became a job

GTM & growth engineering  ·  Jul 2026  ·  4 min read

For most of my career, growth and sales tooling lived in different rooms. Marketing ran campaigns, sales worked the CRM, and someone in the middle exported CSVs between them. GTM engineering is what happens when you stop exporting CSVs and start building the pipe.

The name is new. The work is not. At Merkle I was already automating the boring middle. At Tatvic I wired LinkedIn outreach into the CRM so leads stopped falling through. At Art Galore I ran the entire customer journey on WhatsApp with no-code tools. Same instinct every time: find the manual handoff that breaks, and replace it with a system.

What changed is that the tools finally caught up. Clay, nRev, n8n, and now agents that can browse and write mean one person can run enrichment, scoring, and routing that used to need a team and a quarter.

GTM engineering is mostly refusing to do by hand what a system can do reliably.

So the job, as I do it now, is to own the path from a signal to a seller action. A company shows intent, the system enriches and scores it against ICP, and a rep gets a task with a reason to reach out, the same day. The rep does the judgment. The machine does the repetition.

That is why I spend more time in workflow tools than in slide decks now. The leverage moved.
