How to hire for a GTM team

GTM & growth engineering  ·  Apr 2026  ·  3 min read

I hire GTM people for two things, and neither is on the resume.

The first is reliability. Can I hand them something ambiguous and trust it comes back done, without me checking. On a small team, a dependable person is worth more than a brilliant one who needs managing.

The second is whether they can figure it out. GTM work is full of problems nobody has solved for you yet: a new tool, a broken handoff, a segment you have never sold to. I want people who treat that as the interesting part.

Hire for reliability and resourcefulness. The tools can be taught in a week.

In interviews I skip the trivia and give them a real problem. Here is a messy list and a goal, walk me through how you would get from one to the other. I am not looking for the right answer. I am watching how they break the problem down, where they check their assumptions, and whether they say plainly what they do not know.

The tool knowledge people worry about, the Clays and the CRMs and the ad platforms, is the easy part. Someone reliable who figures things out will learn any stack in a week. The reverse is not true. You cannot teach someone to care that the work is right.
