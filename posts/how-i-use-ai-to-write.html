How I use AI to write

AI is in my drafting, not my thinking. The rules I keep so that what I publish still reads like me.

Notes  ·  Oct 2026  ·  4 min read
TL;DR

I use AI to draft, brainstorm, and proofread, never to decide what to say. I stand behind every sentence I publish. I spend more time authoring than my reader spends reading, because a doc is written once and read many times. If a piece is really just a prompt, I share the prompt.

A quick note on how AI shows up in my writing, because I get asked, and because I publish a fair amount of it here. The short version is that AI sits in my drafting, not my thinking. These are the rules I keep so what goes out still sounds like me and is still mine.

I stand behind every sentence

If a reader points at a line and asks what I meant, the one answer I will not give is that the AI wrote it, ignore it. The moment I publish something, it stands for my thinking. If I cannot defend a sentence, it does not go in. Readers can usually tell the parts you do not mean, and the ones they cannot tell, they take as your real view. Both are a problem.

Writing is thinking, so I do not skip it

Deciding what to emphasize and how to order it is where I actually learn a topic. Hand that to a model to save an hour and I come away understanding the thing less. A spec or a write-up is partly proof that I thought it through. The document is not the only point. The thinking is.

I spend more time writing than you spend reading

A doc is written once and read by many. If I generate a long draft from a short prompt and make you wade through it, I have pushed my work onto your time, multiplied by every reader. Paying the cost once, up front, to make it short and clear is the better trade. And if a piece turns out to be really just the prompt, I will share the prompt.

Where AI helps, and where it does not

AI is good at the average, competent first pass, and that is genuinely useful. It is not good at the calls that make writing worth reading, what to keep, what to cut, what to put your name on. I keep those.

When I quote the machine

Sometimes raw AI output is worth sharing even when it would not pass my bar. That is fine if I label it plainly, like: the model suggested this angle, worth a look? What I will not do is slip unedited output in and present it as my own considered view.

The model can help me write. It does not get to decide what I think.

None of this is anti-AI. I use it every day, including on this site. The line I hold is simple. As these tools get better at reading intent, I will lean on them more, and the line will still hold.
