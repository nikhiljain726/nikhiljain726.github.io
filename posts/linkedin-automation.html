Cutting our paid dependency with LinkedIn automation

GTM & growth engineering  ·  December 2025  ·  5 min read

At Tatvic I came in as Chief of Staff and ended up running marketing. The pipeline leaned hard on paid, which meant growth had a monthly invoice attached and a ceiling we kept paying to lift. The brief I gave myself was simple: build a source of qualified leads that did not switch off the moment we stopped spending.

Organic outbound, run like a system

The lever was LinkedIn, but not the "post more" version. I built an outbound motion: targeted connection and message sequences to the people who actually matched our ICP, tuned for reply quality rather than volume, with the CRM updated automatically so nothing fell through. Within the first three months, qualified lead acquisition roughly doubled, and a growing share of it came from a channel we were not renting.

Paid growth has a monthly invoice and a ceiling you keep paying to lift. Owned channels compound.

Email did more than we gave it credit for

Alongside outbound, I re-engineered how we ran email. It had been an afterthought. Treated properly, as sequences tied to where someone was in the funnel, it grew into about 30 percent of the lead-source mix. Two owned channels working together changed the shape of the whole funnel, and took the pressure off the ad budget.

The unglamorous part: CRM hygiene

None of this holds up without a clean CRM. We tightened lead management so first-contact response times dropped and everyone knew who owned what. That sounds like housekeeping, but it is the difference between a pipeline you can trust and a dashboard you argue about. We also fine-tuned the sales-to-marketing handoff, which lifted discovery-call conversion by 20 to 30 percent. The leads were not suddenly better. We just stopped losing the good ones.

What I took from it

Paid is a fine accelerant and a bad foundation. Owned channels compound, but only if the system behind them, the targeting, the automation, and the CRM, is boring and reliable. Build that, and a small team stops being a function of its ad budget. I do the same thing now, just with different tools. The channels change. The instinct, wire the signal to the action and keep the plumbing boring, does not.
