A performance marketing plan for Meesho, worked end to end

Performance marketing  ·  Mar 2026  ·  6 min read

This is an old assignment I still like, because the answer is a clean example of how I plan paid acquisition. The brief: between January and March 2020, acquire one lakh transactors for Meesho while capping ad spend at 2.5 crore, assuming a 10 percent install-to-transaction rate and an app-only setup. So the target underneath is roughly a million installs inside the budget.

Part A: the channel plan

At a 10 percent install-to-transaction rate, one lakh transactors needs about a million installs. I split the 2.5 crore across three channels by where the volume and efficiency actually were in early 2020, app-first.

  • Facebook, 50 percent (1.25 crore). The workhorse. App-install, conversion, and retargeting at roughly 30 / 40 / 30, so the whole funnel moves rather than buying installs that never transact. Around 4.5 lakh installs.
  • Google UAC, 40 percent (1 crore). Huge reach, cheap installs. V1 install campaigns plus V2 and V3 optimizing on the in-app activation event, A/B tested. Around 3.8 lakh installs.
  • TikTok, 10 percent (25 lakh). A test bet. App-install with in-app event optimization was in beta then, so a small budget to learn a cheap, fast-growing channel. Around 1.2 lakh installs.

That lands near a million installs, which clears the goal with room for the channels that underperform. Split assumptions: male to female 25 / 75, app to web 95 / 5, with web reserved for retargeting people who visited in the last seven days.

The plan starts on benchmarks. The moment real campaign data lands, the mix becomes data-driven and the benchmarks get thrown out.

Campaign setup inside each channel

The structure is built to isolate what works so you can scale it. On Facebook, campaigns are cut by objective, then gender, then persona (interest, behavioural, lookalike, open), then geography (top cities, rest of India, and vernacular state-wise campaigns). Retargeting is its own set: installed but not browsed, browsed but not added, added but not purchased. Personas run from entrepreneurs and working professionals to online shoppers and mid-to-high affinity buyers, with lookalikes built off SDK lists at each funnel stage. Google UAC ramps by month: English and Hindi V1 first, then vernacular and event-optimized V2 and V3, with bids and budgets stepped up 15 percent once the winners are clear.

Part B: the Facebook CPI is 20 percent over plan

The second half of the brief is the part I enjoy: the plan is live and Facebook install cost is running 20 percent above projection. Here is the diagnosis tree I would run.

  • Is it only Facebook, or across channels? If it is everywhere, suspect seasonality or a product change. If it is only Facebook, deep-dive the campaigns.
  • Where in the funnel is the leak? Higher CPMs, lower CTR, higher CPCs, or a drop in install rate. Each points somewhere different.
  • Which campaign type and audience? Install, conversion, or retargeting, and interest, lookalike, or all of them.
  • Are exclusions right? The pixel only tracks about 180 days, so lifetime purchasers leak back in. Exclude them.
  • Creative and placement. Which format and which placement is dragging, and is audience overlap climbing.

The fix follows the finding: eliminate the segment or placement causing the increase, refresh the audience or creative, and relaunch. Same instinct I still run on: isolate the variable, kill what is bleeding, scale what is working.
