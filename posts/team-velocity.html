Building velocity in a GTM team

GTM & growth engineering  ·  May 2026  ·  3 min read

Velocity is not working faster. It is removing the things that make everyone wait.

On a small team, the wait is usually a handoff. Marketing passes a lead and sales does not know why it is good. A rep asks for a list and it takes two days to pull. Someone updates a field and nobody else sees it. Each wait is small. Together they set the pace.

Speed comes from cutting the waits, not from asking people to hurry.

A few things moved the needle for me. Put the reason next to the lead, so a rep can act without a meeting. Make the common list a saved filter, not a request. Keep the CRM clean enough that the pipeline number is trusted on sight. Automate the repetitive middle so people spend their hours on judgment, not data entry.

The other half is a scorecard. What gets measured gets managed, so I track readiness, conversations, and revenue influence in one view, in percentages, and review it every quarter. When the number is visible, the team steers itself. I wrote about that here.

None of this is about tools for their own sake. It is about making the good decision the easy one to repeat, and getting out of people's way while they make it.
