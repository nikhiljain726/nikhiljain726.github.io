On trying things early

Four internships, two pivots, and a startup. The cost of trying is smallest at the start of a career, and it only goes up.

Career & notes  ·  Feb 2024  ·  5 min read

I get asked a version of this a lot. Should I switch domains, should I leave a stable job to build, is it too late to change track. My honest answer is that the cost of trying is smallest at the start of a career, and it only goes up from there. Here is how that played out for me.

Start as a generalist

Before my first full-time role I did four very different internships, and each one shaped how I approach a new problem now.

  1. Data analytics at Gionee. Sales data, inventory placement, working out which stores were worth stocking. My way into analytics.
  2. Technical intern at Phonon. Java and Asterisk, building call-centre systems. It stretched what I thought I could handle technically.
  3. Technical support at Matrix Comsec. Resolving queries for offshore customers. A real lesson in troubleshooting and patience.
  4. Sales trainee at BYJU'S. Door-to-door selling, and the pulse of talking to a customer face to face.

None of these was a straight line, and that is the point. The mix is the reason I do not fear picking up something new. Each turned out to be a stepping stone, even the ones that felt unrelated at the time.

It is fine to explore, especially early

My first job was digital marketing at Merkle Sokrati. I enjoyed it, but I was not sure it was the thing for life, so I went looking. Analytics had pulled at me since college, so I moved to ZS to get a real taste of it. A few months in I had my answer. I wanted to be a growth and marketing person, close to the startups that were most of my clients. So I left ZS and went back to what I enjoyed. On paper that looks like indecision. It was the opposite. I was early enough that trying cost me almost nothing, and it would have been far harder to do after five years in one lane. I am happier for having checked.

Startup vs job, the math

The bigger version of the same question is startup versus job. Say you earn a decent 20 lakh a year. You leave to build your own thing, or you join as a co-founder on someone's idea. Worst case, it does not work and you shut shop in a year. In that year you learn things that would have taken ten in a normal job. Back on the market, more companies want you, because building from the ground up is a rare skill, and the offers are often two to three times your last salary with a broader role. You created enormous value for yourself either way. Compare that to staying put. Would your salary have jumped two to three times in a year? Almost certainly not. And if the startup works, nothing beats it.

That is roughly my own story, except I am still building.

Waiting does not remove the risk of trying. It just makes the risk more expensive.

So if you are early and unsure, that is the good news, not the bad. Take the chance while it is still cheap.
