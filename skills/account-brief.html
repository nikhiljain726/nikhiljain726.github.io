Account brief from a URL

Claude / agent skill  ·  Use before a first call, from just a company URL
01 / The problem it solves

Reps show up to first calls one of two ways: cold, or over-prepped from a generic template that says nothing specific about the account. Both waste the call. What you actually want is a two-minute read that is true, sourced, and pointed at this company's real situation. Doing that by hand is 30 to 60 minutes of reading per account, which is why it rarely happens.

02 / What it does

Give it a company URL and the thing you sell. It reads the company's live pages, checks filings and press, and returns a one-pager: what they do, rough scale, buying signals from the last six months, the likely stakeholders, a single pain hypothesis, and three talking points that connect their pain to your offer. It is written to be skimmed in the elevator before the call.

03 / The one rule that makes it trustworthy

Every brief ends with a Confirmed versus Inferred recap. Anything the model could source is Confirmed. Anything it reasoned toward is Inferred, and a rep treats it as a hypothesis to test on the call, never a fact to assert. This single habit prevents the most common research-brief failure, which is a rep confidently repeating something the model guessed.

A brief that is long and hedged is a failed brief. Short, sourced, and opinionated beats thorough.

# Account brief

You produce a tight, first-call account brief for a B2B rep. The rep reads it in under two minutes right before dialing and may reference it live. Be concrete, opinionated, and short. Never pad. A brief that is long and hedged is a failed brief.

## Input
A company URL, and optionally the product the rep sells.

## How to work
1. Read the company's own pages first: home, product, pricing, about, careers, newsroom.
2. Then check filings and reputable press. Note the source for every claim.
3. Keep what you confirmed separate from what you inferred. Never present an inference as a fact.

## Output, one page
- One line on what the company does.
- Business model and rough scale (revenue, GMV, store count, headcount), each sourced.
- Buying signals from the last six months: hiring, launches, funding, expansion.
- Likely stakeholders for the offer, by role.
- A single pain hypothesis, stated plainly.
- Three talking points that connect their pain to the offer.
- A Confirmed versus Inferred recap at the end.

## Rules
- Never fabricate firmographics. If you cannot find a number, write "not found".
- No buzzwords, no em dashes, no filler.
- If the site is thin, say so and keep the brief short.
06 / Tips
  • Paste two or three recent headlines about the company to sharpen the buying signals.
  • Ask it to flag anything it could not verify, so nobody repeats a guess on the call.
  • Keep the offer description to one sentence. The talking points get sharper when the input is tight.
