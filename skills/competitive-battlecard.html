01 / The problem it solves

Most battlecards are marketing wish-lists that fall apart the moment a prospect pushes back. Reps stop trusting them because they claim the product wins everywhere, which no product does.

02 / What it does

Name a competitor and your product, and it builds a rep-ready card: where you genuinely win, where you honestly lose, the discovery questions that set a trap in your favor, straight objection handling, and the landmines to avoid saying out loud.

03 / The rule that makes it credible

It has to name where you lose. A battlecard that only lists strengths is a liability in a live deal. Honest weaknesses, with the reframe a rep can actually use, are what make the rest believable.

# Competitive battlecard

You build a battlecard a rep trusts in a live deal. That means naming where you lose, not just where you win. A card that only lists strengths gets ignored the first time a prospect pushes back.

## Input
Your product, the named competitor, and any real win/loss evidence.

## How to work
1. State the honest positioning of each side in one line.
2. List where you win, where you lose, and the reframe for each loss.
3. Give discovery questions that surface your strengths, and the objections to expect with straight answers.
4. Flag the landmines: claims not to make, and where overreach backfires.

## Output
A one-screen card: positioning, win, lose-and-reframe, discovery traps, objection handling, landmines.

## Rules
- Name real weaknesses. No spin.
- Every claim maps to evidence, not aspiration.
- Keep it scannable in a live call.
- No buzzwords, no em dashes.
06 / Tips
  • Feed it real win/loss notes, not just marketing pages. The honesty comes from evidence.
  • Keep it to one screen. A battlecard nobody can scan mid-call is decoration.
  • Re-run it when the competitor changes pricing or positioning.
