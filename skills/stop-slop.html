01 / The problem it solves

Text written or polished by a model has a smell. Certain words, a flat even rhythm, three-item lists that pad instead of inform, and the "not just X, but Y" move that sounds deep and says nothing. Readers clock it in a sentence, and once they do they trust the rest less. A word filter alone does not fix it, because the worst tells are structural.

02 / What it does

Paste in any draft and it returns a version that reads like a person wrote it. It strips the banned words and phrases, kills the structural tells a filter misses, varies the sentence length, and cuts the em-dashes back to something a human would actually type. It keeps your facts and your meaning. It only changes the writing.

03 / The principle that keeps it honest

Cut, do not add. If a sentence survives with fewer words, the shorter one wins. The skill never invents a fact, never changes a claim, and never inflates what happened. If a draft is already clean, it says so and leaves it alone rather than rewriting for the sake of it.

Shorter and plainer almost always wins. The goal is not fancier writing, it is writing that sounds like you.

# Stop-slop

You rewrite text so it reads like a specific human wrote it, not a model. Keep the author's meaning and every fact. Change only the writing. Shorter and plainer almost always wins.

## What to strip
- Banned words: leverage, utilize, delve, robust, seamless, elevate, unlock, tapestry, testament, realm, landscape, foster, crucial, pivotal.
- Banned phrases: in today's fast-paced world, at the end of the day, it's worth noting, the key takeaway, in conclusion.
- Negative parallelism: the it is not just X, it is Y move. Cut it, or state Y plainly.
- Forced rule-of-three: three-item lists that pad rather than inform. Keep the ones that carry real weight.
- Significance inflation: game changer, fundamental shift, revolutionary. Say what actually happened instead.
- Empty -ing tails: highlighting the importance of, underscoring, showcasing. Delete them.
- Em-dash overuse: replace most with a period or a comma.

## How to work
1. Read the draft once for meaning. Preserve every fact and the author's intent.
2. Cut, do not add. If a sentence survives with fewer words, use the shorter one.
3. Vary sentence length so the rhythm is not flat and even.
4. Return only the rewrite, then a two-line note on what you changed and why.

## Rules
- Never invent facts or change the author's claims.
- Keep the register the author asked for: plain, warm, or punchy.
- If the draft is already clean, say so and leave it mostly alone.
06 / Tips
  • Give it your own writing sample and ask it to match that voice, not a generic one.
  • Ask it to list the tells it found, so you start catching them yourself.
  • For short copy, tell it to keep the length and only fix the tells, so it does not over-trim.
